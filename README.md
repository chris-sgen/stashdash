# stashdash

> **Unofficial copy — not the website of Stash Dash.** This repository is a development copy of
> a redesign, published so that the redesign can be looked at in a browser. It is not operated by, affiliated with, or endorsed by
> Stash Dash. The business's real site is https://www.stashdash.com/.
>
> **A snapshot, not a shop.** The pages were read from the business's site on the evening of October 12, 2026, California time (October 13, between 00:15 and 02:15 UTC) and are
> not kept current: the products, the prices, the "Out of Stock" labels, the day's deals and the event are
> those the site showed then. **Nothing can be ordered here.** **The subject is cannabis, for adults of 21 and over.**

**Preview: https://chris-sgen.github.io/stashdash/**

A **total redesign** of **stashdash.com** (Stash Dash, which its own site describes as a cannabis retailer and delivery service based in California): 750 pages, made by a rebuild pipeline and changed for
public hosting as listed under *Hardening*. It is a static site with no backend. To look at it, use the preview link. To serve the folder yourself, use a server that answers `/faq` with `faq.html`, under the path `/stashdash/` (the not-found page names its files there); a plain file server breaks the links.
Before it shows a page it asks a visitor to confirm being 21 or older (on 748 of the 750 pages; see *Known limits* for what that question does and does not do).

> **2 pictures and 2 silent video loops in this copy are AI-generated illustrations made for this redesign.**
> They are not photographs of the business, its products or its people. One of the loops stands where the live page shows a photograph (see *Generated imagery*).

Two terms used below. The **private build** is the build of this redesign that this copy was made from,
with its documentation and audit files; it is not published. This **preview** is that build with the
changes listed under *Hardening*.

## Hardening applied to this public copy

`PROVENANCE.json` gives the counts.

1. **`noindex, nofollow, noarchive, nosnippet, noimageindex` on every page.** This tag is the only index control this copy has. It is a request, not a lock: the large search engines say they follow `noindex`; `noimageindex` is a rule of Google's. A `robots.txt` with `Disallow: /` is also shipped, but it has **no effect** here: crawlers read `robots.txt` only at the host root (`chris-sgen.github.io/robots.txt`, which only a separate repository for that host could provide), not under `/stashdash/`. GitHub Pages cannot send an `X-Robots-Tag` header either. That leaves every file that is not a page (the pictures, the loops, the search index, the fonts, the stylesheets, the script, the licence texts and these documents) with no index control of its own; on this site they are linked only from pages marked `nofollow`. The repository itself is public: github.com lists every file of it, and its page there can be indexed.
2. **Link-preview tags.** `og:url` points at this preview, `og:description` / `twitter:description` carry the disclosure, `og:site_name` reads "Unofficial preview (not Stash Dash)", and `og:title` / `twitter:title` begin "Unofficial preview:" (750 pages). The picture tags (`og:image`, `twitter:image`: 1,500 tags) are removed and `twitter:card` is `summary`, the small card, so no tag offers a picture. The business's mark is not this copy's tab or home-screen icon (1,500 icon links removed; an empty icon stands in). **The `<title>` of a page is not marked.** 711 of the 750 titles are the live page's own; 39 are the private build's: 34 products and 1 brand whose live title is a placeholder such as "Meta" (the title is made of the name and the brand), and the not-found page, `/categories`, `/events`, `/search`. 27 titles name the business, 5 of them in a title the build wrote. What a link card shows depends on the app: apps that take a link's description or site name from these tags show the disclosure; apps that read none of these tags show the page's own title with the `chris-sgen.github.io` address; apps that pick a picture from the page itself may show one of its pictures.
3. **JSON-LD removed** (739 blocks). It asserted, as data for search engines, the business's identity, its products with their prices and stock, and the trail of each page.
4. `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` on every page, so a click on an outbound link does not tell its target this address. No page holds a frame, and no page asks another host for a file.
5. **No on-page disclosure banner.** The disclosure is carried by the link-preview tags (in apps that show them; see 2) and by this README. Apart from the notes named here a page looks like a page of the business: its logo, its licence number, its support address, its copyright line and its words. 9 pages carry a note of this preview under their title, before their buttons: `/termsandconditions`, `/privacypolicy`, `/faq`, `/gift-cards`, `/deliverylocations` say that the words are the live site's and describe the live service; `/becomedriver`, `/learnmore` say that the words are the live site's and that what they offer is the business's, as its site stated it on October 12, 2026; `/shop/daily-deals` says that the deals are those the business's site showed on October 12, 2026; `/events` says that the flyer is the business's as its site showed it on October 12, 2026. Every note adds that this preview is a separate redesign; none says "unofficial".
6. `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt` are not shipped, because they advertise the business's real URLs to crawlers. The `_redirects` and `.htaccess` files are not shipped either, since GitHub Pages ignores them. The 47 forwards of the private build therefore do not exist here, and such an address shows the not-found page: 37 `/shop/…` addresses of the live menu's rows, which the rebuild folds into its category pages; 7 `/category/…` addresses, which are `/categories/…` here; and `/brands/CAT4`, `/categories/beverage`, `/submitopportunities`.
7. **`<link rel="canonical">` is kept**, pointing at the business's own address for the page (747 pages). That is the usual signal for a duplicate, and deliberately different from `og:url`; beside `noindex` it is a second, weaker hint in the same direction. `/404.html`, `/categories` and `/search` have none: the search and the not-found page are the build's own, and the live site answers `/categories` with its not-found page. `/brands/cat4` names the live spelling, `/brands/CAT4`. **Description tags:** of 698, 636 are the description the live site gives the page under one of its addresses (8 of them without the sentences named in 10), 61 were put together by the private build from the page's own text where the live page has none or the placeholder "Meta", and 1 is replaced by the disclosure (`/login`: it promises a sign-in to a customer account, which this copy does not have). 1 link whose words are the business's web address opens the live site here (`/gift-cards`); in the private build it leads to the build itself.
8. **Pictures of the live site that this copy does not show** (3, all on `/learnmore`). That page offers the software the service runs on, and illustrates it with three mock-ups of screens. One shows a portrait photograph of a person under a name; one shows a first name, a telephone number and a street address that read like somebody's test entries; one shows a signed-in account and test entries, among them a person's first name. An unaffiliated copy does not republish those. The page is built without the three pictures; its words are all there.
9. **The age question asks for a confirmation of age and for no agreement.** On the live site, and in the private build, the question also has a box to tick, "I agree to Stash Dash's Terms of Use and Privacy Policy", and speaks of "our partner's" terms too. A copy that is not the business's asks nobody to agree to anything: the box, its two links and the refusal shown without it are taken out on all 748 pages that carry the question, and the question keeps the beginning and the end of the live sentence: "You must be 21+ years of age to visit this site." **Its answer is kept to this copy:** "yes" is noted in the browser's local storage under the name `stashdash_preview_age`, so that an answer given on another project of the `chris-sgen.github.io` host does not answer for this one.
10. **Text of the private build that this copy does not carry.** Under every product (634 pages) the live site's two sentences that begin "By clicking below to place your order, you are (i) reaffirming your acceptance of our Terms of Use & Privacy Policy" and end with a line about transportation charges: nothing is ordered here, and the button beside them only opens the product on the live store. On `/events` the words of the event flyer set as text, which the private build prints beside the flyer for readers who cannot see the picture: typed out they are an invitation with a street address, a telephone number, offers and other businesses' names that this copy cannot keep current. The flyer itself is shown, as on the live page, and its description for screen readers still gives the party's name and day. And 9 sentences on 8 product pages that say a product helps with cancer, Parkinson's disease, fibromyalgia, neuropathy or inflammatory bowel disease, or that put a figure on a health effect (also taken out of those pages' description tags). Only those: the Proposition 65 warning that the live site prints, which names cancer, stays, and so do the live site's and its brands' other statements about what a product is used for (stress, pain, sleep, anxiety, depression, nausea, appetite, cramps, migraines, inflammation and the like, and words such as "medication", "therapeutic" and "proven"); see Known limits.
11. **3 buttons lead to the business's own page instead of another site's shop.** On the live `/becomedriver` the buttons "Apply Now" and "Introduce Yourself" lead to `shop.stiiizy.com`; here they open `https://www.stashdash.com/becomedriver`. What follows from an application is for the visitor and the business's own page, not something to start from this copy.
12. **Files left out, files added.** 684 files of the private build that no page, stylesheet or script of this copy names are not shipped: 9 are the three pictures named in 8 in their sizes, 2 the business's icon in two sizes, and 673 are sizes of pictures that only the link-preview tags and the structured data named. The comments of the stylesheet and of the script, notes of the private build to whoever maintains it, are taken out (94 and 31 comments); the script was checked to parse without them. Added: `assets/css/preview.css` (the styles of the notes and of the changed age question), the licence texts of the 3 typefaces beside the font files, `.nojekyll` (GitHub Pages serves the files as they are) and `.gitattributes` (no line endings are rewritten).
13. `404.html` names its files under `/stashdash/`, because GitHub Pages serves it for an unknown address at any depth.

## Known limits

- **Nothing can be ordered here.** The cart, the checkout, the sign-in, the address check, the gift-card purchase and the newsletter sign-up are links to the live store (see *Links that lead to live services* below). This copy has no accounts and no forms but its own search. Its pages set no cookie and keep nothing but the answer to the age question. The host, GitHub Pages, receives every request as any web host does and keeps its own logs; a search sends its words to this copy's own `/search` page as part of the address, so they reach the host too.
- **Not every product of the live store is here.** This copy has the 634 products that the saved pages of the live site link to. The live store's own filter, read in a browser the same evening, counted 681. The difference was not looked into.
- **The age question is a click-through.** It reads "You must be 21+ years of age to visit this site." and takes "I'm at least 21 years old" or "Exit this site" (748 pages; the two documents of the live site, `/termsandconditions` and `/privacypolicy`, have none). It checks no document and verifies nothing. The page is loaded behind it, dimmed and not usable until it is answered. With scripts off it is still asked, on every page anew, and an address that ends in `#gate-passed` shows the page without it. The pictures, the loops and the search index can be opened by their own addresses without the question, and every file can be read in the repository.
- **This is a snapshot of the evening of October 12, 2026 in California (October 13 UTC).** Products, prices, sale prices, potencies, "Out of Stock" labels and the day's deals are those the live site showed then, and a menu of this kind may change daily. The day's deals also stand, by name, in the menu of every page. No product page and no list but the deals page says so: only the notes of `/becomedriver`, `/learnmore`, `/shop/daily-deals` and `/events` name the day. The search index holds the same prices. The business's own site is the place for what holds today.
- **The words on the pages are the live site's, and some of them speak about the live site.** The pages named in Hardening 5 carry a note. Elsewhere the business's words about ordering, accounts, gift cards and delivery are carried as written, without a note: the home page carries 6 questions of `/faq` (the live home page has none) whose answers point to a sign-in by telephone number, an upload of an ID and a selfie, and a chat that only the live site has. This copy takes no orders and delivers nothing.
- **Two pages make offers that only the business can keep.** `/becomedriver` recruits delivery drivers and says what most of them earn; `/learnmore` offers the business's software to other retailers, with figures of its own (a fill rate, a delivery time, the size of its team). Both carry a note. The buttons of `/learnmore` lead to this copy's `/contact`, which shows the business's support address; those of `/becomedriver` open the business's own page (Hardening 11).
- **Statements about health are the live site's and its brands', not advice.** Product and brand descriptions say what a strain or a product is used for, some of them in the words of medicine. They are carried as written, except the sentences named in Hardening 10.
- **The event on `/events` has a date and will pass.** The flyer is a picture; it shows the business's street address, telephone number and social-media name, the names of two bakeries and what is offered to the first customers in line. Its day also stands in a promotion picture on the home page and in the flyer's description for screen readers. The page's note gives the day the flyer was read.
- **The generated pictures and loops are illustrations, not the business's photographs.** No caption or frame on the pages tells them apart; `PROVENANCE.md` lists each one.
- **The video loops have no pause control.** They are silent, hidden from screen readers, and play in place of a still picture on 3 pages (`/`, `/becomedriver`, `/deliverylocations`). When the device asks for reduced motion they do not play at all, and the still picture shows instead. WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) asks for a way to pause moving content that starts by itself and lasts more than five seconds; this design does not provide one.
